# Ashen Company World Creation and the Sundering

**Status:** GM review draft, 4 November 2026. This is proposed setting lore, not approved canon or a player export.

## The First Making

Before there were kingdoms, Bahamut and Tiamat raised the world from the dark between the planes. Bahamut gave it enduring form: stone, deep water, and a sky that would hold. Tiamat gave it change: fire beneath the stone, tides, hunger, and the power to become something new. Neither could make a living world alone. They invited the elder gods to tend its places and people, each accepting a share of the work. The gods did not own the peoples they shaped; they gave them beginnings, then watched them choose their own lives.

The two dragons made the first dragons together. Bahamut favored memory, guardianship, and measured strength. Tiamat favored appetite, adaptation, and the right to seize a place in the world. Their work also brought forth dragonborn, intended to walk among the other peoples and speak to both draconic houses. In this world, dragonborn origin is a settled fact of the myth, although later cultures disagree over which dragon god loved them more.

## The Elder Makers

This table assigns a **primary maker** to each of the ten 2024 Player's Handbook species. It is Ashen Company cosmology, not a claim that the official D&D multiverse has a single creation account. A maker is an ancestral origin, never a moral destiny or required object of worship.

| People | Primary maker in Ashen Company | How the making is understood |
|---|---|---|
| Dragonborn | Bahamut and Tiamat | Brought forth together as the speaking heirs of dragons. |
| Dwarves | Moradin | Raised from living stone and taught to make lasting things. |
| Elves | Corellon | Given forms able to answer beauty, memory, and change. |
| Gnomes | Garl Glittergold | Taught to survive through invention, wit, and hidden roads. |
| Goliaths | Kord | Kord shaped the first giants; goliaths descend from those elder families. The giant lineage remains important. |
| Halflings | Yondalla | Given the art of keeping hearth and community through upheaval. |
| Humans | Pelor | Given short lives, restless ambition, and the capacity to build beyond one generation. |
| Orcs | Gruumsh | Made to range across an unsettled world with endurance and resolve. |
| Aasimar | Pelor | Mortal descendants carrying a lasting spark of the Upper Planes; Pelor did not erase their mortal ancestry. |
| Tieflings | Nerull | In this setting Nerull shaped the first mortal line that later acquired Lower Planes legacies. The three 2024 legacies remain distinct; Nerull did not make every fiend or determine a tiefling's character. |

The last two rows preserve the 2024 distinction between a people first made and a later inherited planar legacy. Nerull as tieflings' first maker is an Ashen-specific invention, not an official D&D origin. Their later Abyssal, Chthonic, or Infernal legacies can still differ.

## The Confrontation

The first disagreement was over what a living world could be allowed to become. Bahamut believed power must remain answerable to those it protected. Tiamat believed no maker had the right to bind creation against its own hunger. Their dispute passed from counsel to war when Tiamat sought to open the world's deep heart and remake the peoples through draconic fire. Bahamut closed it. The two acts struck the same wound from opposite sides.

The result is remembered as **the Sundering of the First Sky**. Mountains broke; seas crossed old shores; entire cities disappeared beneath ash and water. More damaging than the ruin was a fracture in the world's unseen channels. Prayer could no longer cross reliably to a god. Mortal study could no longer hold the great arcane patterns in place. Bargains with powers beyond the world lost their secure path home. Old magic did not all vanish, but the ways of teaching and renewing it were broken.

The elder gods could have stayed and held open those paths. They saw that remaining would draw them into the dragon war and destroy what was left. They fled beyond the broken sky, taking their immediate presence and their living institutions with them. Their peoples survived without them. Temples became shelters, courts, storehouses, and graveyards. Some communities call the flight cowardice; others say it was the last mercy the gods could offer. Both beliefs are credible within the setting.

Bahamut and Tiamat also ceased to answer the world openly after the Sundering. Whether they died, were imprisoned, or withdrew to contain the wound is unknown. Their absence is a central mystery, not a fact the GM must settle now.

## The Four Lost Paths

The following explains why four character traditions disappeared **without making all magic disappear**.

| Lost path | What failed | What remains |
|---|---|---|
| Cleric | The divine channel between god and ordained mortal closed. A temple can preserve prayer and healing knowledge, but cannot train a new cleric by the old rites. | Sacred texts, relics, ordinary healers, and rare unexplained miracles. |
| Paladin | Oaths still matter morally, but the world's broken covenant can no longer carry the oath's supernatural force into a new champion. This is an Ashen setting rule; the 2024 class does not require a god as its ordinary power source. | Orders, vows, armored guardians, and stories of the last oathbearers. |
| Wizard | The great arcane lattice that made spells repeatable through notation and study shattered. A spellbook describes what used to work; copying it no longer makes a wizard. | Dormant libraries, unstable rituals, magical items, and scholars of the old craft. |
| Warlock | The reliable routes for binding a mortal to an otherworldly patron were severed. Powerful beings may still exist and tempt mortals, but a new pact does not grant the old class's repeatable power. | Pact records, debts, cults, and dangerous attempts to reopen a route. |

Bards still work magic through living performance and shared memory. Sorcerers draw on power already present in their blood or being; draconic lines are especially significant. Druids and rangers work through the world's living cycles, which survived the gods' departure. Species traits and old magic items may persist as inherited or stored magic. These distinctions need GM confirmation before class restrictions are treated as rules.

## Present Day Consequences

- Faith remains a culture and personal practice, even when prayers receive no reliable answer. No ancestry is blamed for its maker's departure.
- The last known clerics, paladins, wizards, and warlocks belong to history. **These four classes are unavailable to new player characters**, as directed by the GM. Relics associated with them are valuable, politically dangerous, and often misunderstood.
- Dragonborn carry a contested inheritance from both dragon makers. Neither color nor lineage determines allegiance.
- Archives and ruins may preserve contradictory accounts of the Sundering. A church account, a dragonborn oral history, and a wizard's damaged record can each hold part of the truth.
- The Ashen Company can encounter clues to the lost paths through missions. Such discoveries do not unlock these classes for new characters under the current campaign rule.

## Hooks for Later Missions

1. A sealed Redwater ledger lists supplies sent to a chapel that officially burned generations ago; its cellar contains an oath register with one name written after the Sundering.
2. A dragonborn witness identifies two incompatible versions of the same creation hymn, each naming a different cause of the catastrophe.
3. A spellbook recovered from a flooded library changes its diagrams when placed beside the Company banner. This is a clue, not proof that wizardry has returned.
4. A tiefling family claims an elder maker's mark alongside its planar legacy, challenging the claim that any people were wholly abandoned.

## Canon and Rules Decisions Before Approval

1. **Class access decided:** Cleric, Paladin, Wizard, and Warlock are unavailable to new player characters. The earlier restricted labels for three of them must be retired in campaign guidance. Lectern does not yet enforce class access during character entry or import.
2. **The dragon gods' fate:** Decide whether their absence remains a permanent mystery or has a private GM answer.
3. **The Sundering's age:** Set a rough era only when it matters for surviving ruins, family histories, and The Lost Banner.
4. **Publication:** Split approved player history from any GM-only truth before adding it to Lectern. The current campaign root is GM-visible, so a public lore record or a deliberate visibility change is needed before this material can appear in the printable player guide.
